# TEWA Storytelling: Capture → Structure → Share

A thinking framework for documenting technical work with credibility, transparency, and human impact.

## What This Skill Does

Transforms a project or learning into a **TEWA narrative**—a structured story suitable for LinkedIn, portfolios, or public documentation:

- **T (Topic)**: What you built and where it can be used
- **E (Experience)**: What you learned and why you're credible
- **W (Work)**: The actual process—tools, iterations, and what worked
- **A (Art)**: The human story—why it matters, who it helps, your voice

Every section includes **credits**—citing sources, frameworks, and inspirations—which is the foundation of trustworthiness.

## Core Principles

1. **Credit is debt**—you cite sources, research, and influences. No shortcuts.
2. **Real work over polish**—show the iterations, failures, and pivots. That's the story.
3. **Human at the center**—explain the problem you solved *for real people*, not abstractions.
4. **Machine-readable**—structure it so agents can parse, remix, and redistribute your work.

## When to Use This Skill

You have a project or learning you want to **share widely**, and you want it to:
- Stand up to credibility checks (proper citations)
- Showcase your thinking process (not just the result)
- Connect emotionally with your audience (human impact)
- Be repurposable (one document → multiple posts, email pitches, portfolio entries)

**This is NOT for**:
- Quick social media thoughts (use your normal voice)
- Private work logs (those don't need the full structure)
- Topics where you don't have substantive depth (TEWA requires real experience + research)

## The TEWA Workflow

### Step 1: Capture Your Project (5 minutes)

Start with raw thoughts. Don't overthink structure yet.

**Ask human:**
- What problem does this solve? (A—personal issue)
- What tools/frameworks did I use? (W—work)
- What research or concepts back this up? (E—experience)
- What's the core idea? (T—topic)


**Write rough answers to:**
```
Real problem solved: [what human problem or inefficiency]
Tools/tech used: [list]
Key learning: [what you discovered]
Core concept: [1-2 sentences of what this is]
```

### Step 2: Structure into TEWA Format

Use the **TEWA Schema** (see below). Fill in each section:

#### T — TOPIC (The What & Where)
- **Core concept**: Define the idea in 1-2 sentences. Then explain deeper.
- **Historical context**: Where did this idea come from? Who pioneered similar work?
- **Current applications**: Where is this being used now?
- **Implementation strategies**: Hard (requires infrastructure), Soft (mindset/process), Smart (leverage existing systems)
- **Credits**: Who/what inspired or informed the core concept?

**LinkedIn Angle**: The "what" hook—why should people care about this concept?

#### E — EXPERIENCE (Your Credibility)
- **What you learned**: Be specific about your knowledge level (BS vs PhD understanding)
- **What research says**: Cite studies, papers, industry findings
- **What you discovered**: Original insight not in existing research
- **Comparison matrix**: How does your approach vs alternatives?
- **Credits**: Researchers, mentors, communities that shaped your thinking

**LinkedIn Angle**: The "why you're credible" proof—you did the homework.

#### W — WORK (The Real Process)
- **Implementation process**: High-level summary + timeline
- **Documentation**: Screenshots, videos, code snippets, architecture diagrams
- **Tech stack**: Tools used + why chosen
- **What already existed**: Frameworks/libraries you leveraged
- **What helped**: Blockers, solutions, key decisions
- **Iterations**: How many tries? What failed? What worked?
- **Code/assets**: Links to GitHub, demos, live examples
- **Credits**: Tools, tutorials, people who unblocked you

**LinkedIn Angle**: The "how I actually built it" story—messy, iterative, real.

#### A — ART (The Human Story)
- **Personal issue solved**: The real human problem (not abstract)
- **Design philosophy**: Principles guiding your choices
- **Beauty & craft**: Why the solution is thoughtfully built
- **Personal voice**: How this reflects your values/aesthetic
- **Impact & meaning**: What does this represent?
- **Portfolio presentation**: Hero image/video + one-liner hook + narrative arc
- **Public links**: Demo, repo, blog post, social posts
- **Credits**: Design inspiration, mentors, collaborators

**LinkedIn Angle**: The "why this matters" emotional hook—connect to their life.

### Step 3: Fill in Substantive Detail

Don't skimp. Agents and readers can tell when you're phoning it in.

- **Citations**: Link to papers, articles, GitHub repos, people you learned from. Real URLs.
- **Iterations**: List actual version numbers or dates. "Tried 3 times" → "V1: tried Firebase (too slow), V2: tried Supabase (better), V3: PostgreSQL (production-ready)"
- **Code links**: Public repo or gist. If it's private, describe the architecture in words.
- **Visual proof**: At minimum, 1 screenshot or video showing it working.

### Step 4: Fact-Check & Credit

Before publishing:
- [ ] Every claim has a source (paper, tutorial, person, company)
- [ ] Every tool/framework credits the creator
- [ ] Every insight credits the research or person who discovered it first
- [ ] Links are live and verified
- [ ] You haven't overstated what you learned or discovered

**The debt principle**: You're standing on the work of others. Settle that debt explicitly.

### Step 5: Publish in Multiple Formats

One TEWA document → multiple outputs:

#### LinkedIn Post (Formatted TEWA)
- Open with A (personal story hook)
- Explain T (core concept, 2-3 lines)
- Show E (credibility, your learning)
- Describe W (tools, process, iterations)
- Close with why it matters
- Link to full blog post or repo

**Structure:**
```
[Hook—human problem you solved]

I built [project name] to solve [specific issue].

Here's what I learned:

T — The Concept:
[1-2 paragraph core concept + applications]

E — What I Discovered:
[Key learning + what research says + your original insight]

W — How I Built It:
[Tech stack + iterations + what worked]

A — Why It Matters:
[Personal connection + impact]

[Link to full write-up, repo, demo]

Thanks to [people/projects/mentors who helped].
```

#### Blog Post (Full TEWA + Narrative)
- Weave TEWA sections into narrative prose
- Include images, code snippets, metrics
- Deep-dive on what failed + why
- Full citations and further reading

#### Portfolio Entry
- Screenshot + one-liner hook
- A (personal problem) + W (what you built)
- Links to live demo, repo, blog post

#### Email Pitch (TEWA Compressed)
- Subject: [A—personal hook]
- Body: T (concept) → E (credibility) → W (proof) → CTA
- Footer: Credits/links

## The TEWA Schema (Detailed)

```
PROJECT NAME
├── T_TOPIC
│   ├── core_concept (definition + expanded)
│   ├── historical_context (origins + evolution)
│   ├── current_applications (3-5 real examples)
│   ├── implementation_strategies
│   │   ├── hard_implementation (infrastructure-heavy)
│   │   ├── soft_implementation (mindset/process)
│   │   └── smart_implementation (leverage existing)
│   └── credits (concept origins + sources)
│
├── E_EXPERIENCE
│   ├── personal_learning (what you learned + proof level)
│   ├── research_findings (studies + credibility sources)
│   ├── original_discoveries (insight not in research)
│   ├── comparison_matrix (this vs alternatives)
│   └── credits (researchers + mentors + institutions)
│
├── W_WORK
│   ├── implementation_process (problem + timeline)
│   ├── documentation (videos + screenshots + diagrams)
│   ├── tools_and_tech_stack (why each tool)
│   ├── what_already_existed (frameworks you leveraged)
│   ├── what_helped (blockers + solutions + decisions)
│   ├── iterations (v1→v2→v3, what failed, what worked)
│   ├── code_and_assets (GitHub + demos + configs)
│   └── credits (tools + tutorials + people)
│
└── A_ART
    ├── personal_issue_solved (human problem)
    ├── design_philosophy (your principles)
    ├── beauty_and_craft (thoughtful choices)
    ├── personal_voice (your aesthetic + values)
    ├── impact_and_meaning (what it represents)
    ├── portfolio_presentation (hook + hero image + narrative)
    ├── public_links (demo + repo + blog + posts)
    └── credits (inspiration + collaborators)
```
